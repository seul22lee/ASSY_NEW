# Substitution examples

Six evidence-grounded substitution assessments, demonstrating the general model
in `ontology/core/substitution.ttl`. **These are demonstrations of the model, not
the central scope of the ontology.**

Source data: `data/substitutions.yaml`. Built into
`ontology/alignments.ttl`. Query them with
`python3 scripts/query_examples.py --query 6`.

Every assessment states: the preserved function, the operating context, the
conditions, the requirements, the modifications, the trade-offs, the failure
implications, the verification needed, and its evidence.

**All six are at `mdcore:NeedsReview`.** None has been signed off by a human
engineer.

| ID | Candidate replaces baseline | Context | Verdict | Confidence |

| SA-001 | Involute spline ← parallel key | hub slides under torque | `PreferredAlternative` | 0.85 |

| SA-002 | Press fit ← parallel key | hub fixed, steady torque | `ConditionallySubstitutable` | 0.75 |
| SA-003 | Setscrew ← parallel key | low torque, adjustable | `ConditionallySubstitutable` | 0.70 |
| SA-004 | Two keys ← one key | hub length limits key length | `ConditionallySubstitutable` | 0.80 |
| SA-005 | Cylindrical roller ← deep-groove ball | radial **and thrust** load | `NotAnAlternative` | 0.70 |

**No assessment reaches `DirectlySubstitutable`.** Every pair needs at least a
change of shaft or hub feature. That is the honest result of the pilot, not a
gap in it.

## SA-001 / SA-006 — the asymmetry demonstration

These two assess **the same pair of alternatives in the same context, in
opposite directions**, and reach opposite conclusions. They exist to prove the
model does not treat substitutability as symmetric.

### SA-001 — Involute spline replaces parallel key

| | |
|---|---|
| **Preserved function** | `mech:TransmitTorqueShaftToHub` |
| **Context** | `ctx-axial-slide-under-torque` — hub must slide axially along the shaft *while transmitting torque*; continuous rotation; torsional loading |
| **Verdict** | `PreferredAlternative` |
| **Interface** | `InterfaceCompatibleWithRework` |

**Conditions**

1. The requirement for axial travel under torque is what makes the spline
   preferred; without it the assessment does not apply.
   *Mott ch. 11 sec. 11-5 p. 479; Shigley ch. 7 sec. 7-3 p. 357.*
2. The spline must be specified with a fit that permits sliding — Shigley's
   "reasonably loose slip fit", or Mott's SAE fit class C (movement under load).
   *Shigley p. 357; Mott p. 480.*

**Requirements** — applicable: torque capacity, axial motion, manufacturing
cost, concentricity. Satisfied: torque capacity, axial motion, concentricity.
**Violated: manufacturing cost.**

**Required modifications**

| Modification | Effort | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| Replace the shaft keyseat with an external spline, generated by hobbing | significant | source-derived (Mott p. 479) |
| Replace the hub keyway with a matching internal spline | significant | **engineering inference** |
| Specify the spline to a published SAE or ANSI standard, including fit class | moderate | source-derived (Shigley p. 357; Mott p. 480) |

**Advantages** (all source-derived)

- *Motion allowance — fully satisfies.* A spline with a loose slip fit allows
  large axial motion while still transmitting torque; a standard parallel key
  must not be allowed to slide. *Shigley p. 357; Mott p. 479.*
- *Load capacity — fully satisfies.* With four or more splines rather than one
  or two keys, torque transfer is more uniform and loading on any given part of
  the interface is lower. *Mott p. 479.*
- *Alignment — fully satisfies.* The involute form self-centres the mating
  element. *Mott p. 479.*

**Disadvantage** — *Cost — does not satisfy.* "Splines are generally much more
expensive to manufacture than keys." *Shigley p. 357.*

**Trade-off** — the capability to slide under torque is bought with a much more
expensive shaft and hub feature. Where no axial motion is needed, the same
source calls a spline "usually not necessary."

**Failure implications**

| | Failure mode | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Introduced | `mech:WearOut` — a sliding spline interface wears; Mott notes the surface is often hardened precisely for axial-motion service | Mott p. 479 |
| Introduced | `mech:FatigueFracture` — stress concentration is greatest where the spline blends into the shaft, though described as "generally quite moderate" | Shigley p. 357 |
| Mitigated | `mech:ShearFailure` — the single key's shear plane is removed | Mott p. 479 |
| Mitigated | `mech:FrettingWear` — splines are integral with the shaft, so the relative motion possible between a key and its keyseat cannot occur | Mott p. 479 |

**Verification required**

1. Spline torque capacity, Mott Eq. (11–9)/(11–10) — **inch units, and stated
   per inch of engaged length**. *Mott p. 480.*
2. Stress-concentration assessment where the spline runs out into the shaft.
3. Fit and tolerance inspection confirming the achieved fit permits the required
   sliding.

**Analyst note.** Neither book states this substitution. Mott rules the key out
for sliding service; Shigley rules the spline in. The conclusion is an analyst
synthesis over two cited claims and is marked `NeedsReview`.

### SA-006 — Parallel key replaces involute spline (same context)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Verdict** | `NotAnAlternative` |
| **Interface** | `InterfaceIncompatible` |
| **Violated requirement** | axial motion |

**Condition.** In this context the hub must slide under torque — which is
exactly what a standard parallel key must not be permitted to do. *Mott p. 479.*

**Why this matters.** SA-001 and SA-006 share a pair and a context and disagree.
A graph that inferred the reverse edge from the forward one would assert
something both sources contradict. `mdcore:substitutionAssessedAs` is therefore
not `owl:SymmetricProperty`, and `validate_ontology.py` fails the build if a
mirrored edge exists without its own assessment.

## SA-002 — Interference (press or shrink) fit replaces parallel key

| | |
|---|---|
| **Context** | `ctx-steady-fixed-hub` — hub permanently located, steady unidirectional torque, no field disassembly |
| **Verdict** | `ConditionallySubstitutable` |
| **Interface** | `InterfaceCompatibleWithRework` |

**Conditions**

1. The design torque must not exceed the friction capacity computed at the
   **minimum** interference — that is what bounds the torque the joint may be
   designed to transmit without slipping. *Shigley ch. 7 sec. 7-8 p. 392.*
2. No requirement for routine field disassembly. *(engineering inference)*
3. The interface pressure assumed in the capacity calculation must be maintained
   in service; the capacity is a friction capacity and depends on it.
   *(normalized interpretation of Shigley p. 392)*

**Requirements** — satisfied: axial location, fatigue life. **Violated: field
disassembly, overload protection.**

**Modifications** — delete keyseat and keyway (Shigley p. 390); specify a fit
class with tolerances on both members (p. 391); specify the assembly method,
press or thermal *(inference)*.

**Advantages**

- *Life — partially satisfies.* Removing the keyseat removes a stress raiser at
  which fatigue cracks characteristically initiate; the press-fit
  stress-concentration factor is described as "usually quite small."
  *Shigley p. 357; Mott p. 176.*
- *Positioning — fully satisfies.* Press and shrink fits serve both torque
  transfer and axial location, and "can sometimes be used effectively to
  minimize the need for shoulders and keyways." *Shigley pp. 357, 390.*

**Disadvantages**

- *Maintainability — does not satisfy.* Not separable with ordinary tooling; the
  source offers a split clamping hub as the variant that does allow disassembly.
  *Shigley p. 357.*
- *Overload protection — does not satisfy.* The connection no longer contains a
  sacrificial member. Where the key was intended to fail before costlier
  components, that behaviour is lost. *Shigley p. 383.*

**Failure implications**

| | Failure mode | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Introduced | `mech:ConnectionSlip` — capacity is frictional, so gross slip replaces member failure as the governing mode | Shigley p. 392 |
| Introduced | `mech:FatigueFracture` — stress concentration in the shaft bending stress near the hub ends, from the sudden change between compressed and uncompressed material; first estimates "typically not greater than 2", and the source declines to generalise | Shigley p. 392 |
| Mitigated | `mech:ShearFailure` — no key to shear *(inference)* |
| Mitigated | `mech:BearingCompressionFailure` — no key flanks to crush *(inference)* |

**Verification required**

1. Interface pressure from the interference, using **maximum** interference to
   check for excessive stress. *Shigley Eqs. (7–39)/(7–40), p. 391.*
2. Torque capacity `T = (π/2)·f·p·l·d²` at **minimum** interference.
   *Shigley Eqs. (7–48)/(7–49), p. 392.*
3. Fit and tolerance inspection.
4. **Assembly torque test** — `testRecommended: true`,
   `testProcedureSpecified: false`, `acceptanceCriterionSpecified: false`.
   Requires manufacturer data or an experimental protocol.

**The decisive open item.** Shigley's equation is complete in form but the cited
section **does not fix the coefficient of friction *f***. The torque capacity is
therefore *not determinable from these two books alone*. Recorded as a gap
requiring manufacturer data or test — **not** filled with a value from elsewhere.

## SA-003 — Setscrew replaces parallel key at low torque

| | |
|---|---|
| **Context** | `ctx-low-torque-adjustable` — low torque, hub position adjusted during assembly, cost-driven |
| **Verdict** | `ConditionallySubstitutable`, with an `InsufficientEvidence` sub-finding |

**Conditions**

1. Applicable only to "cases of low torque transmission" — the source lists
   setscrews in hubs among the means available for such cases.
   *Shigley ch. 7 sec. 7-3 p. 357.*
2. Holding power derives from frictional resistance plus slight penetration of
   the screw into the shaft, so the seating torque must be achieved and
   retained. *Shigley pp. 380, 382.*

**Advantages** — no keyseat to machine *(inference)*; resists axial motion of
the collar or hub, tabulated as holding power *(Shigley pp. 380, 382)*.

**Disadvantage** — *torque capacity — weakly satisfies.* The source recommends
factors of safety of **4 to 8 for various dynamic loads**, against **1.5 to 2.0
for static loads**. *Shigley p. 382.*

**Trade-off** — the cheapest connection is also the one the source confines to
low torque and derates most heavily under dynamic load.

**Introduced failure modes** — `mech:ConnectionSlip` (frictional transfer);
`mech:Loosening` (holding power depends on a retained seating torque).

**Unresolved — `InsufficientEvidence`.** Neither source defines a numeric
boundary for "low torque transmission," so **the applicability condition of this
assessment cannot be evaluated automatically.** Rule `SUB-006` degrades exactly
this case to `InsufficientEvidence` for machine evaluation while retaining the
human-readable conditional verdict.

This is the model behaving correctly. A pipeline that invented a torque
threshold to make the rule executable would be fabricating a design limit.

## SA-004 — Two keys replace one key when the hub is too short

| | |
|---|---|
| **Context** | `ctx-hub-length-limited` — required key length exceeds available hub length |
| **Verdict** | `ConditionallySubstitutable` |

**Condition.** Applies where the computed minimum key length exceeds the hub
length — the situation Mott's parallel-key design procedure addresses at step 7,
offering "two keys or a spline." *Mott p. 477.*

**Modification.** Cut a second keyseat in shaft and hub, typically 90° from the
first. *Shigley p. 383.*

**Advantage** — *torque capacity — fully satisfies.* "Multiple keys may be used
as necessary to carry greater loads." *Shigley p. 383.*

**Disadvantage** — *life — partially satisfies.* A second keyseat is a second
stress raiser, at locations where fatigue cracks characteristically initiate.
*(engineering inference, citing Mott p. 176)*

**Unresolved — `InsufficientEvidence`.** **Neither source states how torque
divides between two keys.** Equal sharing must not be assumed.

This is the most quietly useful finding in the set. "Two keys each carry half
the torque" is a frequently made and rarely justified assumption. The assessment
records the gap rather than closing it.

## SA-005 — Cylindrical roller bearing replaces deep-groove ball bearing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Preserved function** | `mech:SupportRotatingShaft` |
| **Context** | `ctx-radial-and-thrust-support` — combined radial and thrust load |
| **Verdict** | `NotAnAlternative` |
| **Violated requirement** | carry thrust load |

**Disadvantage** — *load capacity — does not satisfy.* The candidate does not
perform `mech:CarryThrustLoad` in this ontology. Shigley describes **tapered**
roller bearings, not cylindrical ones, as taking "either radial or thrust loads
or any combination of the two." *Shigley ch. 11 sec. 11-1 p. 565.*

**Verification, if the context were relaxed to radial load only.** The
rating-life calculation must be re-run **with the roller exponent, not the ball
exponent**. *Shigley p. 566; Mott p. 571.* This is rule `SUB-007`: substituting
across a load-life exponent boundary invalidates the baseline life calculation.

**Unresolved — `InsufficientEvidence`.** The cited sections **do not state a
thrust capacity for cylindrical roller bearings**. The exclusion rests on this
ontology's function assignment plus Shigley's contrasting description of tapered
roller bearings — *not* on a direct statement that cylindrical roller bearings
cannot take thrust.

Included deliberately to show a `NotAnAlternative` verdict that rests partly on
ontology structure rather than wholly on cited text, and to show that such a
verdict is **flagged as evidence-limited** rather than presented as
textbook-supported.

## What the six assessments demonstrate

1. **Context decides.** SA-001 and SA-006 differ only in direction and reach
   opposite verdicts.
2. **Substitution is not symmetric**, and the model refuses to infer the reverse
   edge.
3. **Substitution is not transitive**, and no transitive closure is materialised.
4. **A shared function is not enough.** All six pairs share a function; none is
   `DirectlySubstitutable`.
5. **Conditions are mandatory.** SHACL rejects a conditional verdict with no
   condition and no modification.
6. **Gaps are recorded, not filled.** Three `InsufficientEvidence` findings: an
   unquantified "low torque", an unknown two-key load split, an unstated thrust
   capacity. Plus one open parameter — the friction coefficient *f* — without
   which SA-002 cannot be closed from these two books.
7. **Analyst inference is labelled.** Every advantage, condition and modification
   carries a `mdcore:valueProvenance`, so nothing inferred can be quoted as
   textbook content.
8. **A stronger connection is not automatically better.** SA-002 loses the
   sacrificial-member behaviour that Shigley says is desirable in a key.
