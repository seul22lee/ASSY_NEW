# EXE-BM002-01 — MuJoCo simulation inputs

This directory holds the **inputs** to the BM-002 rigid-body dynamics run. The
outputs are in `../validation/simulation/`.

Everything here is generated by `../simulate_lift.py` from the **accepted** CAD.
No CAD was changed to build it: the geometry signature
`6824e5102424e3db883f16b684ab54f02c14eed19bead0116c704092156bc2ee` is verified
against a fresh rebuild before the first model is written, and the run refuses to
proceed if it does not match.

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `model_empty.xml` | MJCF for the empty-platform scenario |
| `model_payload_1kg.xml` | MJCF with SCENARIO-PAYLOAD-1KG merged into the platform |
| `mass_properties.json` | every product body's CAD volume, centre of mass, mass and inertia tensor, plus the declared densities |
| `model_mapping.json` | how each CAD product body appears in the model, and what that topology can and cannot support |
| `simulation_parameters.yaml` | solver, integrator, timestep, gravity, actuator law, sampling, and the full assumption set |

## The topology, and why it is this one

| CAD body | MuJoCo | DOF |
|---|---|---|
| `BODY-HOUSING` | `housing_and_panel`, welded to the world | 0 |
| `BODY-REAR-PANEL` | same body — rigidly fixed to the housing in the completed product | 0 |
| `BODY-CRANK-SHAFT` | `crank`, hinge about **+X at the CAD crank axis** y = 70, z = 60 mm | 1 |
| `BODY-CRANK-JOINT-PIN` | welded into `crank` | 0 |
| `BODY-CONNECTING-ROD` | `rod`, hinge about **+X at the CAD crank-pin axis** | 1 |
| `BODY-PLATFORM` | `platform`, slide along **+Z** | 1 |
| `BODY-PLATFORM-JOINT-PIN` | welded into `platform` | 0 |
| `SCENARIO-PAYLOAD-1KG` | extra inertial merged into `platform`, **payload model only** | 0 |

The far end of the rod is tied to the platform by a MuJoCo **equality connect** at
the CAD platform-pin axis. A slider-crank is a closed loop and MuJoCo integrates a
tree, so the loop has to be closed by a constraint. `connect` fixes the two bodies
to share that point and leaves their relative rotation free, which in this planar
mechanism is exactly a pin joint.

Three joint DOF minus two independent in-plane constraint rows leaves **one net
degree of freedom** — the crank angle, which is what the actuator drives.

**The mechanism is modelled, not the answer.** There is no crank-angle-to-platform
-height equation anywhere in the model. The platform's height is whatever the
solver produces from the crank, the rod and the constraints. The analytic
slider-crank relation appears only in `simulate_lift.py` as an *independent*
cross-check, re-derived there from R, L and the axis height so that the two sides
of the comparison are not the same function.

### The two welds

Each joint pin is a body of revolution about a joint axis parallel to X, and each
pin's centre is rigidly fixed to its parent in the CAD. Welding is therefore
**exact** for position and for inertia about the joint axis — a pin's own spin
carries no dynamics here. Both pins keep their full CAD mass and inertia, merged
into the parent by the parallel-axis theorem. This makes **no** claim about pin
contact, bending or bearing stress.

## Declared assumptions

The source states no material (DOS-BM-002 S3), so densities are chosen and
labelled:

| class | density | bodies |
|---|---|---|
| polymer-like | **1200 kg/m³** | HOUSING, REAR-PANEL, PLATFORM |
| metal-like | **7850 kg/m³** | CRANK-SHAFT, CONNECTING-ROD, both PINS |

> **DECLARED SIMULATION ASSUMPTION. NOT A SOURCE REQUIREMENT. NOT A VERIFIED
> MATERIAL SELECTION.**

Masses follow from `CAD volume × declared density`, so the **absolute** empty and
payload torques are material-assumption-dependent and scale with these numbers.
The **incremental** payload torque — the payload run minus the empty run — does
not, because the only thing that differs between the two runs is the 1.000 kg
scenario payload. That is the robust result, and it is the one cross-checked
against an independent analytic expression.

Gravity is `[0, 0, −9.81] m/s²`. Joint damping and joint friction are **zero** in
the primary model; a 0.01 N·m·s/rad variant is run as a declared sensitivity.

## What this model can and cannot carry

**It can support:** rigid-body dynamics of the assembled mechanism; actuator
torque under the declared assumptions; back-driving tendency when the actuator is
released; **ideal** joint and guide reactions; numerical constraint stability.

**It cannot support:** contact-level jamming, guide wear, tolerance binding, local
guide pressure, manufacturing variation, frictional wedging, or any stress, strain
or strength result.

The guide is a **single ideal prismatic constraint**. The real CAD guide is two
channels with 0.2 mm side and 0.4 mm tip clearances. Nothing in this model
resolves contact, so:

* **REQ-007 (jamming) stays NOT_VERIFIED** — NRM-BM-002-014 and NEG-BM-002-011.
* **REQ-003 (payload capacity) stays UNSUPPORTED** — a constraint reaction is a
  resultant, not a stress, and no stress is computed anywhere (UNR-BM-002-007).

Joint reaction outputs are labelled **IDEAL JOINT / CONSTRAINT REACTION**
throughout. They are never called contact pressure, bearing stress, pin stress or
guide contact stress.

## Reproducing

```
~/.venvs/ver3cad/bin/python ../simulate_lift.py            # full run, with videos
~/.venvs/ver3cad/bin/python ../simulate_lift.py --quick --no-video
```

The run writes both MJCF files, all reports under `../validation/simulation/`,
seven plots, three videos and an artifact-hash manifest. The MJCF files are
regenerated each time from the CAD, so editing them by hand has no effect on the
results — change `simulate_lift.py` or the CAD instead.
